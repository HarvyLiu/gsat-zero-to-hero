## Marking it yourself

Check your answers only when you have finished. Work out your raw score (out of $100$) like this:

| Item type | How it scores |
|---|---|
| Single choice (1–7, 18) | Full points if correct (items 1–7 are $5$ points each, item 18 is $3$), otherwise $0$ |
| Multiple select (8–12) | $5$ points each; count the options you got wrong, $k$, and score $\frac{5-2k}{5}\times 5$, with a negative result counted as $0$; a blank item also scores $0$ |
| Fill-in (13–17) | $5$ points each, only if every cell is right |
| Written (19, 20) | $6$ points each, criterion by criterion against the marking scheme |

Mark the written items honestly. The CEEC Math B paper says to show your working or reasons, that answers are graded on the reasoning, and that no process or a wrong inference scores zero. The 115 Math A marking principles add that full marks need correct reasoning, a detailed process and the right answer; correct reasoning that does not reach the final answer earns partial credit; and no process, a wrong inference, or data that contradicts the problem scores zero. So:

- An answer with no working scores $0$, even if the answer is right.
- Clear reasoning from correct premises that slips at the end still earns the points for the earlier steps.
- Don't award yourself points for steps the criteria don't list.

A raw score is not a level <Zh>級分</Zh>. The GSAT converts scores like this: <Fact id="exam.level-scale" /> The step depends on that year's actual candidates, so this practice paper's score cannot be turned into a level; use it to find your weak spots.
