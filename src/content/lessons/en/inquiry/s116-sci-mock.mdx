This is the last chapter of the 116 science sprint, for students who have already studied the four required sciences and inquiry and practice (<Zh>探究與實作</Zh>) in school. The earlier chapters reviewed one topic at a time, and the previous one, the [exam toolkit](/en/inquiry/s116-sci-exam-toolkit/), gathered the tested skills beyond the textbook; this one puts them together in a whole paper.

## Instructions

This is an **original** practice paper: every item was written for this site, and none is a CEEC exam item; the experimental settings and their data are invented illustrative data, and the constants an item gives are the usual textbook values. The layout is based on the 111–115 Science papers and CEEC's item-feature articles; **the 116 paper has not been published, so its layout is not confirmed**. The brochure leaves scoring and full marks to each paper, so $128$ points is the custom of recent years, not a rule.

Read the table: Part 1 (items 1–36) is worth $72$ points and Part 2 (items 37–56) $56$, $128$ in all. Part 2 has $12$ choice items ($24$ points) and $8$ written items ($32$ points), the same split as in 115.

This layout matches the real papers in some ways and differs in others:

- Part 1 has one $9$-item block per subject, in an order that rotates by year; in 115 it was earth science 1–9, physics 10–18, chemistry 19–27 and biology 28–36. This paper uses the order physics, chemistry, biology, earth science, which is just one of the possible orders.
- In 115 the Part 2 sets were 37–39 biology, 40–43 biology and chemistry, 44–46 chemistry, 47–49 physics, 50–53 earth science with physics, and 54–56 earth science. This paper keeps the same item ranges and only swaps the places of the physics and biology single-subject sets.
- From 112 to 115, Part 2 had one earth-science-only set and one earth science cross-subject set every year; items 54–56 and 50–53 of this paper follow that pattern.
- The 116 brochure says physics, chemistry, biology and earth science carry roughly equal shares of the points, and that the paper includes inquiry and practice content. Here each subject has $26$ points of single-subject items, and the two cross-subject sets carry $12$ points each.

Rules for this paper:

- **Time:** $110$ minutes. Time yourself, and don't look anything up or check the answers along the way.
- **Answer on paper first.** Enter the choice items only after finishing the whole paper; don't open the hints, and score your first submission.
- **No calculator.** The exam-room rule: <Fact id="exam.calculators" />
- **Single choice** scores only when correct; a wrong answer or more than one mark scores zero.
- **Multiple select:** <Fact id="exam.multi-select-scoring" />
- **Item sets:** the shared description, table and data are in the first sub-item; later sub-items say "(Continuing item N.)", so look back when you answer them.
- **Items 39, 42, 43, 46, 49, 52, 53 and 56 are written items:** write out your working or reasons on paper, then mark yourself against the rubric.
- Every constant and value an item needs is given in that item.

## Marking yourself

Check the answers only after you finish. Work out your raw score (out of $128$) like this:

| Item type | How it is scored |
|---|---|
| Single choice | $2$ points each: full marks if correct, otherwise $0$ |
| Multiple select | $2$ points each: count the options you judged wrongly, $k$, and score $\frac{5-2k}{5}\times 2$, with a negative result counted as $0$; an item left blank scores $0$ |
| Written (39, 42, 43, 46, 49, 52, 53, 56) | $4$ points each, marked criterion by criterion against the rubric |

Every multiple-select item in this paper has $5$ options. Under this rule, an item with $k$ options judged wrongly scores:

| Options judged wrongly, $k$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Score | $2$ | $1.2$ | $0.4$ | $0$ |

Mark the written items honestly. CEEC's marking principles ask for your calculations or reasons, and a bare answer can score zero, as in 115 item 53. Whether partial credit is given is decided by each item's own marking principles, not by a general rule, as 111 item 57 shows. Every rubric in this paper has four criteria worth $1$ point each, so:

- A criterion that needs working or a reason scores nothing for a bare answer, even a correct one.
- If your early steps are right and a later step goes wrong, you keep the points for the early criteria.
- Don't give yourself points for anything the rubric does not mention; different wording with the same meaning counts.

A raw score is not a level score. GSAT raw scores become levels like this: <Fact id="exam.level-scale" /> The step depends on how that year's candidates actually scored, so your score on this practice paper cannot be turned into a level. Use it to find your weak spots.
